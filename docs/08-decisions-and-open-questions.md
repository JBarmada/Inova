# Decisions and Open Questions

Discussion record, November 22, 2026. This separates explicit preferences from proposals and unresolved mechanics.

# Established direction from the team discussion

- The project is named Inova, with repository `JBarmada/Inova`.
- The companion is a pixel dog.
- The app should feel like a game, including sound, animation, and effects.
- Adoption should use a lightweight dog picker with an adoption-center feel.
- The everyday dog should float over desktop windows, have no visible rectangular background, and be draggable.
- Expressions and emotions are distinct from clothing and props.
- Clothes and props should use reusable layers to support combinations.
- Cosmetics unlock through experiences and use.
- Experience-based interaction items and an expanding world are desired.
- A dated, readable bookshelf should let the user revisit memories that can also inform the dog.

# Proposed implementation scope

The minimum scope, initial asset counts, fixed reward rules, and manual-input-first plan are working proposals. Team size, deadline, budget, operating system, and required technologies have not yet been supplied.

# Zombie and revival idea

The team suggested a comical secret or optional mode: the dog becomes a zombie or skeleton, and a revival potion or lightning brings it back. Another example was eye bags after an all-nighter.

Two possible triggers were discussed: a special mode and a response to missed commitments. Neither is finalized. This updates the earlier blanket proposal that the dog never has a death-like transformation.

My recommendation for discussion: make spooky transformations an optional playful mode, preserve earned items and memories, and let the user exit it without earning permission to recover. The team's alternative of earning a revival through renewed commitment remains recorded. Choose the mechanic before implementation.

# Product principles to review with the team

Keep effort and recovery rewarding. Let users pause or quiet the companion. Make all-night humor cosmetic rather than an incentive to sacrifice sleep. Explain what the app knows and let users correct or remove it. Keep the dog's reactions supportive even when the student is struggling.
